## Intro

Most of the matter you have ever encountered is made of atoms: nuclei surrounded by electrons. Crush matter hard enough and that comfortable picture breaks down.

## What We Think

At the pressures inside a collapsed star, matter may rearrange into phases that have no stable existence anywhere we can build. One long-standing idea is that, under the right conditions, matter containing strange quarks could be more stable than ordinary nuclear matter. If true, it would mean the periodic table is a local habit, not a universal law.

## What We Do Not Know

We cannot make this matter and keep it. Colliders create hot, dense fireballs that exist for an instant; that is a different regime from the cold, ultra-dense, long-lived matter a star might hold. Whether stable exotic phases exist at all remains open.

## Why It Matters

If there are stable forms of matter we have never produced, then our laboratory inventory of "what matter can be" is incomplete. The cosmos may be running experiments we cannot.
